What happens after you drop off your finished 7” x 9” square at the Pickcode booth?

Every square donated to Blanket Statement becomes part of a larger community quilt. In this post, we take a look behind the scenes at how hundreds of individual contributions become cozy blankets for **Project Linus**.

![Yarn logo](/img/yarn-logo.jpg)

## About Project Linus

[Project Linus](https://www.projectlinus.org/) is a national non-profit organization that provides handmade blankets to children who are seriously ill, traumatized, or otherwise in need. Since its founding in 1995, Project Linus has delivered more than **10 million blankets** nationwide.

## The Journey of a Blanket Block

1. **Collection**: Squares are gathered from conference participants, logged, and categorized by color palette.
2. **Layout & Arrangement**: Volunteers arrange 24 to 35 squares into attractive patchwork designs (usually 4x6 or 5x7 grids).
3. **Seaming**: The blocks are crocheted or mattress-stitched together with coordinating yarn.
4. **Bordering**: A clean decorative border (single crochet or crab stitch) is added around the perimeter for durability and finish.
5. **Quality Check & Labeling**: Each blanket receives an official Project Linus label and is inspected for safety (secure tails, softness, washability).
6. **Delivery**: Local chapters deliver the blankets to pediatric hospitals, shelters, and crisis centers.

## How You Can Help Beyond the Conference

Can't attend in person? You can knit or crochet blocks at home and mail them to us, or start a Blanket Statement chapter at your own local meetup or school!
